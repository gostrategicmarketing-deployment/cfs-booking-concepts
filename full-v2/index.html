This Review Is Built for Families Like Yours

It will be most useful if:

  • Your household earns about $250,000 or more, or pays about $30,000 or more a year in federal taxes.
  • You expect to help pay for college.
  • You have college costs coming up or already underway.
  • You have savings, investments, retirement contributions, or annual cash flow that should be looked at together.
  • You want to weigh suitability, risk, liquidity, timing, and tax consequences before acting.

It's Probably Not a Fit If

  • You don't expect to help pay for college.
  • You're looking for guaranteed tax savings, investment returns, cash flow, or financial aid.
  • You want to act on a tax or investment idea without professional review.

Common Questions

Will this really work for someone like me?

That's exactly why the first conversation exists.

I'm a W-2 employee. Does this still apply?

It can. The framework includes tax-planning concepts that may apply to W-2 employees, 1099 earners, and business owners. Whether a strategy fits depends on your situation and the rules involved.

Do I have to leave my CPA?

Not necessarily. Where it makes sense, your existing CPA can stay part of the process.

I don't have time for another project.

Neither do most of the families we work with. That's one of the reasons our model exists.

How much money do I need?

Some strategies require meaningful capital. Others have different requirements. The first conversation is where we look at what resources are available and whether these strategies are realistic for your situation.
